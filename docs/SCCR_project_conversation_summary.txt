Important terminology clarified
-------------------------------
OCPD = Overcurrent Protective Device. In this project that typically means a device that opens a circuit during an overcurrent, short circuit, or ground fault.

Examples:
- Fuse: Class J, Class CC, LPJ, LP-CC, FNQ-R, etc.
- Circuit breaker: UL 489 breaker.
- MCP / instantaneous-trip breaker when used in a listed combination motor controller.
- Self-protected combination motor controller.

Important distinction:
- Fuse/breaker interrupting rating = how much fault current the OCPD can safely interrupt.
- Component SCCR = how much fault current the component can safely withstand.
- Panel SCCR is generally limited by the weakest relevant component or OCPD unless a valid UL 508A modification or manufacturer combination rating applies.

Key calculation/workflow logic captured
---------------------------------------
The working logic for the Excel/workflow/app concept was based on these ideas:

1. Identify all relevant power circuit components and control-circuit OCPDs.
2. Separate feeder circuits from branch circuits.
   - Feeder circuit: supply side of the branch circuit OCPD, typically incoming terminals/disconnect/distribution blocks up to the line side of branch OCPDs.
   - Branch circuit: after the final branch circuit OCPD protecting the load.
3. Determine component SCCR by one of:
   - Manufacturer marking.
   - Manufacturer instructions/datasheet.
   - Manufacturer tested combination rating with specified fuse/breaker.
   - UL 508A SB4.1 default rating for unmarked listed/recognized components.
4. Determine branch SCCR.
   - Usually the minimum of load-side component SCCRs and branch OCPD rating/interrupting rating, subject to valid combination ratings.
5. Determine feeder SCCR.
   - Usually the minimum SCCR of feeder components, unless a valid current-limiting feeder OCPD rule applies.
6. Apply current-limiting feeder fuse logic only where valid.
   - Feeder fuse must be a valid current-limiting type such as Class CC, G, J, L, RK1, RK5, or T.
   - Use fuse class/amp let-through values at selected available fault current, e.g. 50 kA, 100 kA, or 200 kA.
   - Downstream component SCCR must be at least the peak let-through current.
   - Do not use upstream devices to raise an OCPD interrupting rating.
   - Do not treat this as series rating.
7. Transformer logic needs special handling.
   - Small isolated-secondary transformer circuits may be treated using UL 508A SB transformer rules, depending on kVA/secondary/impedance and downstream component/OCPD ratings.
8. Overall panel SCCR is the lowest applicable value after the branch/feeder/current-limiting/transformer rules.
9. Produce an audit trail that identifies the limiting row/device and why.

Important cautions
------------------
- Any tool/workbook/app created here is a calculation aid, not a UL-certified product.
- Final interpretation should be checked against UL 508A and the AHJ/UL inspector/company compliance process.
- Manufacturer combination ratings must be followed exactly: specific device, fuse/breaker type, maximum amp rating, voltage, installation conditions, and sometimes specific accessories.
- Slash voltage ratings such as 480Y/277 V may be more restrictive than straight 480 V ratings.
- Control-circuit OCPDs can limit the panel SCCR if their interrupting rating is low.
- One-port SPDs may be exempt from SCCR depending on UL 508A rules; do not assume all SPDs are exempt without verifying device/application.
- Filters, power supplies, transformers, reactors, current transformers, resistors, varistors, and voltmeters may have special/exempt handling depending on UL 508A SB4.2.1 and application. Verify before excluding them.
